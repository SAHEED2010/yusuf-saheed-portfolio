## Mission

Help build a clear, credible personal portfolio for Yusuf Saheed. The portfolio should make the owner's strengths, selected work, evidence, and next action easy to understand. It should feel intentional and personal, not like a generic AI-generated template.

The confirmed product scope is a full-stack portfolio with authenticated content administration and an AI assistant. The portfolio content must be editable without code changes. The assistant's public and private capabilities, publishing safeguards, and knowledge sources must be resolved during discovery before implementation.

The visitor experience should provide five curated suggested questions and allow at most five custom questions per visitor in a rolling 24-hour period. The admin assistant must create reviewable drafts and require explicit confirmation before publishing. Approved social profiles may be read as public sources, but private messages and autonomous social publishing are out of scope.

## AI Hero workflow

Follow this order for meaningful work:

1. **Idea**: restate the requested outcome and identify the smallest useful scope.
2. **Grilling**: ask focused questions about goals, audience, content, hierarchy, constraints, and success. Resolve low-fidelity decisions before implementation. Do not pretend a visual or interaction question is settled when it needs a prototype.
3. **Research**: when external facts, standards, or tooling are needed, use high-trust primary sources and record links and dates. Research informs the decision; it does not replace the owner's judgment.
4. **Prototype**: for high-fidelity layout, interaction, or architecture questions, build the smallest throwaway experiment, inspect it, and carry the decision back into the main work.
5. **Specification**: once the direction is understood, write the destination as a concise spec in `.scratch/<feature-slug>/spec.md`. Describe user-visible behavior and acceptance criteria, not speculative implementation detail.
6. **Tickets**: split the spec into independently verifiable vertical slices under `.scratch/<feature-slug>/issues/`. Record blocking relationships. Prefer tracer bullets that cross the full experience over large horizontal layers.
7. **Implementation**: claim the next unblocked ticket, make one small change, and use the repository's existing tools and conventions. Test behavior at the real boundary whenever practical.
8. **Feedback**: run the narrowest useful automated check after each slice. For visual work, inspect the rendered result at desktop and mobile sizes. Fix failures before expanding scope.
9. **Review**: compare the result with the spec, accessibility expectations, security, maintainability, and the owner's stated taste. Human approval is required for irreversible or externally visible actions.

## Context discipline

- Keep the current scope small enough to stay in the model's smart zone.
- Preserve decisions in the spec, tickets, `CONTEXT.md`, or an ADR before clearing context.
- Use a handoff when work moves to another session or repository context.
- Stop planning when the remaining questions are implementation-level and the next tracer bullet can answer them.

## Portfolio standards

- Lead with the owner's real identity, capabilities, and strongest evidence.
- Use real project details and outcomes; never fabricate accomplishments.
- Make navigation, contact, and calls to action obvious without overwhelming the visitor.
- Prefer a few strong case studies over a catalogue of shallow cards.
- Keep content, design, and technology decisions explainable.
- Preserve accessibility, responsive behavior, performance, and readable content hierarchy.
- Do not add analytics, forms, integrations, deployment, or third-party services without explicit approval.
